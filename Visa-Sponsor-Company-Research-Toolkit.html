2Companies-by-Sector Guide

Not all industries sponsor visas equally. Some hire hundreds of graduates on visa every year. Others almost never do. Your first job is to stop wasting time on industries that do not sponsor, and focus all your energy on the sectors where visa sponsorship is the default, not the exception.

Tech companies sponsor the most visas. They need engineers and developers and they hire globally because the local talent pool is smaller than their need. Cloud platforms, software houses, AI firms, data companies, and fintech all have established visa sponsorship processes. They know visa requirements. They budget for visa costs. When you apply to a tech company, you are applying to a place that sees visa sponsorship as a normal part of hiring.

Professional services firms, consulting houses, and financial institutions also sponsor regularly. They have graduate programs designed for people on visa. They have HR teams with visa expertise. They hire for client delivery, so they need to scale quickly and often cannot fill roles locally fast enough.

Some sectors almost never sponsor graduates. Retail, hospitality, small family businesses, and local government rarely have the visa budget or the process to sponsor someone new. Do not waste time here. Your toolkit includes a full sector breakdown so you know where to focus your outreach.

  • Tech and software: highest visa sponsorship, standardised process, large graduate programs
  • Professional services and consulting: established visa budgets, graduate hiring programs, fast-track visa approvals
  • Financial institutions: visa-friendly, strong grad programs, clear sponsorship criteria
  • Avoid: retail, hospitality, small businesses, government agencies without tech focus
Keep in mind: If a company has a grad program, it almost always sponsors visa. Check their careers page and search for 'graduate program' plus the company name.

3Research Template: How to Screen a Company for Visa Sponsorship Appetite

Before you apply or reach out, you need to know if this company will sponsor you. A company with visa appetite will show three clear signals: they post jobs for graduates, they mention visa or sponsorship on their careers page, and they have hired graduates on visa recently.

Start by visiting the company careers page and search for the word 'sponsor'. If you find it, they sponsor. If their grad program exists, they sponsor. If you see job postings that say 'graduates welcome' or 'visa support available', they sponsor. These companies are telling you upfront that visa is not a problem.

Next, check their LinkedIn. Search for people who joined in the past 12 months from outside the country. If you find 3 or more, the company hires internationally. Look at their job titles. Are they graduates? Are they in engineering, data, or product roles? This tells you the company hires visa graduates in your target role.

The final signal is recent news. Search 'Company Name graduate visa sponsorship' on Google. Recent articles, blog posts, or LinkedIn updates that mention hiring or visa support confirm the company is actively sponsoring right now. An old article from 2022 does not tell you what the company does in 2025.

  • Check their careers page for 'sponsor', 'visa', or 'graduate program'
  • Search LinkedIn for recent hires from outside the country in your target role
  • Google the company plus 'visa' or 'graduate sponsorship' for recent news
  • Look for 3 or more signals before you invest time in outreach
Keep in mind: If you cannot find three signals, move to the next company. Do not reach out to companies with weak signals. Your time is too valuable.
  • Careers page mentions visa sponsorship or has a graduate program
  • Company has hired 3+ people internationally in the past 12 months
  • LinkedIn shows recent posts about hiring or graduate programs
  • Google search finds recent news about their visa sponsorship or hiring

4LinkedIn Search Filters and Boolean Search Strings

LinkedIn is where hiring managers live. But searching LinkedIn without a strategy wastes hours. You will search through thousands of profiles and find nothing. The right search strings find the right person in minutes.

Here is how search strings work. You type into the LinkedIn search bar specific words and symbols that tell LinkedIn to find profiles that match exactly what you need. For example, if you search 'title:"Hiring Manager" AND company:TechCorp AND started:2024', LinkedIn will show you people who have the job title Hiring Manager, work at TechCorp, and started there in 2024. This is a warm connection because they just joined and they know how the hiring process works.

Use the boolean search strings in your toolkit for each role you target. Boolean search means using AND, OR, and NOT to combine search terms. For example: title:("Engineering Manager" OR "Tech Lead") AND company:("Company A" OR "Company B") finds managers at either company. Change the company names to the ones you are targeting. Change the job titles to match the hiring managers for your role.

LinkedIn also has native filters in the sidebar. You can filter by company, job title, location, when they joined, and whether they post frequently. Use filters first to narrow the pool, then use boolean search strings to find the exact person. A person who joined recently and posts about hiring is your ideal target because they are active, they remember the hiring process, and you can see their voice on your feed.

  • Use boolean operators (AND, OR, NOT) to build exact search strings
  • Search for recently joined hiring managers: they remember the hiring process
  • Look for people who post about hiring or team growth on their feed
  • Filter by company first, then use search strings to find your target person
  • Target job titles: Hiring Manager, Talent Lead, Tech Recruiter, Engineering Manager, Team Lead
Keep in mind: A hiring manager who has posted in the past month is 10 times more likely to reply to your outreach than someone inactive. Check their feed before you reach out.

5Outreach Tracker: Never Lose Track of Your Target Companies

You will reach out to 20 to 30 companies over 8 to 12 weeks. Without a tracker, you will forget who you contacted, when you sent the message, and when you need to follow up. You will send duplicate messages. You will miss follow-up opportunities. A tracker fixes this.

Your toolkit includes a spreadsheet tracker with columns for company name, hiring manager name, date you sent your first outreach, message type (cold, warm, LinkedIn), when you need to follow up, and notes about the conversation. Every time you reach out to someone, add one row. Every week, filter by 'follow-up date' and see who you need to message again.

The tracker also includes a status column. Mark each person as 'No Reply', 'Replied', 'Interview Scheduled', or 'Rejected'. This tells you at a glance how many people are warm vs cold. If you have been tracking for 4 weeks and everyone is 'No Reply', your message needs to change, or your target companies need to change.

Fill in the tracker as you go. Do not do 50 outreaches and then try to remember who you contacted. One row takes 30 seconds. Do it immediately after you hit send. The tracker is not busywork. It is your proof that you have a system and you are making progress.

  • Add one row every time you send an outreach message
  • Update status weekly based on replies
  • Filter by follow-up date to know who to contact this week
  • Review your tracker weekly to spot patterns: which message types get replies, which companies are warm
Keep in mind: If someone does not reply after three outreaches spaced 2 weeks apart, move on. Do not get stuck on one person. There are 100 other hiring managers who will reply.
  • Spreadsheet has columns: Company, Hiring Manager, Date Sent, Message Type, Follow-up Date, Status, Notes

6Email Templates: Five Ready-to-Send Outreach Messages

Most people freeze when they try to write an outreach email. They think it has to be perfect. They rewrite it 10 times. They never send it. Your toolkit includes five templates you can copy and send today. All you need to do is fill in the hiring manager name, company name, and specific detail about why you are interested.

The cold outreach email assumes the hiring manager does not know you. You found them on LinkedIn. You have no mutual connection. This template is short, specific, and asks for 15 minutes of their time. Do not ask for a job. Ask to learn about their hiring plans and their grad sponsorship process.

The warm introduction template is for when you have a mutual friend. Your friend introduced you or gave you the hiring manager name. This template thanks the mutual friend, explains your background quickly, and asks for a short conversation. Warm outreaches get 5 times more replies than cold.

The after-meeting template is for when you have already spoken to someone. You thanked them verbally. Now you are sending a follow-up email to confirm what you discussed, remind them of your interest, and ask what the next step is. This template keeps you top of mind after the call ends.

The LinkedIn message template is shorter because LinkedIn messages are easier to ignore than email. Make it personal. Reference something specific they posted or something about their company. End with a clear ask: 15 minutes on a call or a reply with next steps. Do not send a generic 'Let me know if you are hiring' message. You will get ignored.

  • Cold outreach: 'I found you, I respect your work, can we chat for 15 minutes?'
  • Warm intro: 'Person X suggested we talk, here is why I am interested, when are you free?'
  • After meeting: 'Here is what we discussed, here is my next step, when should we connect again?'
  • LinkedIn message: Personal, specific, and with a clear ask for a short call
  • Generic templates: Always add one detail that shows you did research on their company
Keep in mind: Send templates as-is. Do not overthink it. A good outreach email that is sent today beats a perfect email that is never sent.

7Monthly Check-In Guide: Keep Your Outreach Moving Without Burning Out

Outreach is a marathon, not a sprint. You will send dozens of messages over 8 to 12 weeks. If you do not pace yourself, you will burn out. You will stop reaching out. You will tell yourself 'this is not working' and give up. Your monthly check-in guide helps you stay consistent without exhausting yourself.

Every month, block 90 minutes on your calendar. This is your outreach sprint. During this time, you will review your tracker, decide which 10 to 15 companies to target that month, research those companies using your research template, find the hiring managers on LinkedIn, and send your outreach messages. Then you are done for two weeks. Do not reach out every single day. You will burn out.

Between sprints, you spend 15 minutes a week updating your tracker and sending one follow-up message to someone who is warm (someone who replied or agreed to chat). This keeps conversations moving without overwhelming you. The rule is simple: one new outreach sprint a month, one follow-up a week.

At the end of each month, look at your tracker and count replies. How many people replied? How many agreed to a call? What message got the best response rate? If cold outreach is failing but warm introductions are working, spend next month building your warm network. If your research is showing the wrong companies, refine your company list. Your tracker tells you what is working. Listen to it.

Set a realistic target. If you are studying full time, you cannot do 20 outreaches a month. Do 10. If you can do 2 hours a week, do 15. The goal is consistency, not volume. One good outreach a week, done for 12 weeks, beats 50 desperate messages sent all at once.

  • Schedule one 90-minute outreach sprint each month
  • Research 10 to 15 companies and send outreach messages during your sprint
  • Between sprints, spend 15 minutes a week following up with warm contacts
  • Review your tracker monthly and adjust which companies to target based on what is working
  • Track your reply rate, meeting rate, and offer rate so you can improve
Keep in mind: If you feel burned out, reduce your target. Consistency beats heroic bursts. You only need one yes.
